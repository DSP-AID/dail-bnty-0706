The 2026 SMU Mustangs football team is who represents Southern Methodist University (SMU) as a member of the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC) during the 2026 NCAA Division I FBS football season. The program is led by fifth-year head coach Rhett Lashlee, the Mustangs play home games at Gerald J. Ford Stadium in University Park, Texas.

SMU defeated Florida State 27–24 on Labor Day. Shortly before the start of the game, Doak Campbell Stadium suffered a major power outage. The outage was unrelated to any inclement weather, creating uncertainty as to if it would be restored quickly enough to play the game that night. Administrators from both schools and the ACC met throughout the outage to discuss contingency plans for a possible postponement, as well as for how to operate the game if the stadium were to have power properly restored. An hour and 40 minutes after the game's scheduled kick time, the game began with no scoreboard, no coach-to-player headset communication, and no video review. Midway through the second quarter, most of the stadium's remaining electrical systems came online, and the game proceeded as any other for the rest of its duration.[9]

SMU's victory came despite several miscues throughout the game. The team trailed the turnover margin 4–0,[a] with two interceptions, a muffed punt, and a lost fumble. The team's defense largely prevented Florida State from capitalizing on these errors, keeping the game close, although an offside penalty on a field goal try allowed Florida State to score a touchdown to tie the game 17–17 in the third quarter.[10] Florida State only managed to convert two out fifteen third down plays.[11] SMU's offense, led by veteran quarterback Kevin Jennings, was explosive throughout the game, totaling nearly 600 yards, including several long touchdown passes. This continued entering the fourth quarter, with SMU quickly scoring to retake their lead, but Florida State quickly answered with their own touchdown to tie the game once again 24–24. SMU's subsequent drive was cut short by Jennings' second interception of the night. After regaining the ball once again with the game still tied, SMU approached field goal range and began running out the clock before kicking a go-ahead field goal with 25 seconds on the clock. Florida State failed to advance at all on their final drive, and the game ended after SMU intercepted a desperation lateral pass, their only turnover of the night.[12]

SMU lost to Louisville in a 41–31 shootout, dealing a significant blow to the team's chances of making the ACC championship game. Often tied during the first half, the teams traded the lead five times throughout the second half of the game until Louisville pulled away in the fourth quarter with a ten-point lead. Both teams leaned significantly on their running game, with Cal transfer Kendrick Raphael recording career highs in both rushing yards and touchdowns. Defensively, SMU allowed Louisville to make several breakaway running plays, contributing to their defeat.[14][15]

In addition to committing three turnovers, the team also failed two fourth down conversions. For the first, early in the game, SMU opted against punting on Louisville's side of the field. Louisville capitalized on the mistake, taking the lead soon after. The other took place as SMU attempted to make a comeback during the fourth quarter, where Kevin Jennings ran with the ball and failed to make the conversion, allowing Louisville to take a two-score lead soon after. Jennings committed two further turnovers while attempting to mount a comeback, a consistent issue throughout his career. While running the ball himself for a touchdown, Louisville forced a fumble at the one yard line, and the ball fell out of the endzone for a touchback, and on the team's final possession, Jennings threw an interception, sealing SMU's defeat.[16]

SMU defeated Missouri State 34–24 in a game highly reminiscent of the team's first meeting in 2025. Like in 2025, Missouri State, now led by former SMU offensive coordinator Casey Woods, took a 10–0 lead in the first quarter before SMU took control of the game and won. Unlike in 2025, however, after SMU took the lead, Missouri State kept the game close in an attempted fourth quarter comeback, but they fell short and lost by 10 points.[17] SMU continued its struggles with turnovers that had haunted them all season, with a pick-six thrown by Jennings and a red zone fumble by Yamir Knight contributing to Missouri State's early lead. SMU already had one of the worst turnover margins of any FBS team entering the game and had yet to win the turnover margin against any FBS opponent.[18] Kicking proved another point of struggle for SMU, as it had in 2025 where they lost three games by three points or less. SMU missed both of its field goal attempts, each from a different placekicker, and they missed an extra point due to a bad snap and hold.[19]

During the game, Kevin Jennings broke four more school records. He broke two records previously held by Ben Hicks, surpassing 9,081 career passing yards and beating his career completions record. He also broke Tanner Mordecai's record of 78 total career touchdowns and Josh McCown's record of 19 consecutive completions, throwing 21 straight.[17]

SMU defeated Boston College 25–16, severely underperforming pregame expectations. SMU's offense struggled in the rain, gaining less than half of their average yardage during the course of the game. SMU defeated Virginia 23–13 in a much cleaner game than previous victories. SMU's first drive ended with a turnover on downs on their own 34-yard line after a dropped deep pass, but Virginia fumbled the ball near the endzone, committing the game's only turnover. Tied 10–10 on the last play of the first half, a penalty by Virginia put SMU in field goal range, allowing them to take the lead entering halftime. Special teams improved substantially compared to previous games, with former SMU soccer player Brendan Baird making all three attempted field goals throughout the game. A fourth quarter touchdown reception by Jordan Hale extended SMU's lead to two scores, a margin they held for the rest of the game.[21]

SMU routed Cal 59–20 after having entered halftime down 17–20. The teams appeared nearly evenly matched during the first half, with several mistakes holding back SMU, such as allowing Cal to convert a first down on a fake field goal that eventually led to a touchdown. Cal rallied behind quarterback Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele, who returned from an injury to throw for over 200 yards in the first half, and the team kicked a field goal as time expired in the first half to take the lead. Starting in the second half, however, SMU completely stifled Sagapolutele and the rest of Cal's offense, shutting them out, while SMU's offense scored touchdowns on six consecutive drives.[22] On the second touchdown of the half, Kevin Jennings threw his 73rd career touchdown pass, breaking SMU's career passing touchdown record previously held by Tanner Mordecai.[23] Jennings also finished his third game in a row without throwing an interception, his longest stretch of doing so since 2024, and a marked improvement on the five interceptions he had thrown in the previous four games this season.[24] Kendrick Raphael, a former Cal Golden Bear, led SMU's rushing offense with over 100 yards, but freshman running backs Christian Rhodes and EJ Kelly also saw significant action, scoring three rushing touchdowns.[25]

SMU lost to Syracuse 27–30. Their sluggish defense allowed Syracuse to take a 20–10 lead at halftime, including three points following a fumble by receiver Jalen Hale. Special teams penalties proved particularly costly to SMU, with a facemask penalty giving Syracuse good field position on one drive and a blindside block penalty negating a 50-yard return that would have started a drive on Syracuse's 8-yard line. Syracuse also sacked Kevin Jennings four times throughout the game, more than in any previous game all season. SMU's offense reignited in the second half, taking their own 27–20 lead with 9 minutes to go in the fourth quarter. On their final offensive drive, however, a dropped pass by tight end Theo Melin Öhrström forced a punt, after which Syracuse ran down the remainder of the clock and scored a touchdown with only 17 seconds left, taking a 3-point lead. SMU's desperation drive sputtered, with Syracuse intercepting a lateral pass on the final play of the game to seal their victory.[26][27]

SMU's second conference loss significantly hampers their ability to make the ACC championship game, requiring the team to win all of its remaining conference games and likely forcing them to rely on tiebreakers among the other two-loss ACC teams.[28]

Wake Forest routed SMU 49–7, effectively ending SMU's chances at making either the ACC Championship or the College Football Playoff. It was the team's first home conference loss since joining the ACC and the team's first double-digit loss at home since losing to Cincinnati 42–13 in 2020.

The game went poorly from SMU right at the start with their first two drives ending in a punt and an interception, respectively. Throughout the rest of the half, SMU committed two turnovers on downs and lost a fumble. Additionally, Wake Forest eviscerated the defense, particularly on the ground, taking a 42–0 lead by the end of the first half, including two dropped interceptions. Despite normally allowing among the fewest third-down conversions of any FBS team, SMU allowed Wake Forest to convert 10 out of 15 attempts this game. Safety Ahmaad Moses was able to return to the team after resolving a dispute over his eligibility, but this made little difference. While both the offense and defense improved in the second half, finally scoring a touchdown and holding Wake Forest to only one of their own, the team's deficit was far too large to recover from.[31]
