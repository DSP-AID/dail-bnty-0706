The 2026 UCLA Bruins football team represents the University of California, Los Angeles as a member of the Big Ten Conference during the 2026 NCAA Division I FBS football season.[1] They are led by first-year head coach Bob Chesney, and play their home games at Rose Bowl located in Pasadena, California.[2][3] It is the Bruins' 107th season overall and 3rd as a member of the Big Ten.[4] Coming off a 3–9 record in 2025,[5] they began the season 9–0 and ranked No. 4 in the Associated Press (AP) poll, their highest ranking since reaching No. 4 after a 6–0 start in 2001.[6] UCLA opened the season with Nico Iamaleava as their starting quarterback, but he was lost for the season after a leg injury in week 5 against Oregon. He was replaced by Ty Dieffenbach.[7]

Rankings

[edit]
Further information: 2026 NCAA Division I FBS football rankings

UCLA began the season unranked before entering the AP poll at No. 23 following a 4–0 start.[9] After improving to 8–0, the Bruins were No. 8 in the initial College Football Playoff (CFP) rankings. It tied the program's then-highest CFP ranking, set in 2014.[10] Their road win over Minnesota the following week pushed UCLA to No. 4 in the CFP rankings.[11][12]

In UCLA's Big Ten opener, Knight ran for 177 yards and two touchdowns and Nico Iamaleava passed for 276 yards and three touchdowns in a 52–28 win over Purdue.[16] Woods ran for a 16-yard touchdown on the Bruins first drive of the third quarter to help them regain the lead,[16] and they pulled away in the second half.[17] UCLA improved to 3–0, matching their win total from the previous season.[17] Chesney became the third Bruins coach in the last 50 years to begin their tenure 3–0, joining Terry Donahue (1976) and Jim Mora (2012).[16]

Knight ran for a touchdown and caught a pass for another, while the Bruins defense forced seven turnovers, as No. 21 UCLA upset the No. 13 Oregon Ducks 17–15.[22][23] It ended a five-game losing streak against Oregon, and it was the Bruins' first road win against the Ducks since 2004.[23] It was also their first win on the road against a ranked opponent since 2019.[24] Chesney became the first coach to begin their UCLA career 5–0,[23] and it was the program's first 5–0 start since 2022.[25]

In the game's opening series, the Bruins drove to the Ducks two-yard line, before settling on a 21-yard field goal by Mateen Bhaghani. After Iamaleava was sacked and fumbled on the UCLA 1, the Ducks recovered the ball and scored on a one-yard run by Jordon Davison to go up 6–3.[22] Following a sack and fumble by Oregon quarterback Dylan Raiola, Knight scored on a 35-yard run to give the Bruins a 10–6 lead. They failed to extend their lead in the first half despite outgaining the Ducks 241–58 in total yards, including a 162–36 advantage in passing yards and 79–22 in rushing.[23] Iamaleava fumbled once and threw two interceptions. He injured his right leg late in the second quarter, which sidelined him in the second half. He was replaced by backup Ty Dieffenbach, who threw a nine-yard touchdown to Knight for a 17–6 lead with 13 minutes remaining in the game.[23]

An Oregon touchdown with 2:16 remaining brought the score to 17–15. The Ducks attempted a two-point conversion to tie the game. Game officials spotted the ball at half the distance from the line of scrimmage. instead of spotting the ball half the distance from the end of the previous play, which had ended in a facemask penalty by the Bruins.[26] The Bruins stopped the second two-point attempt by the Ducks from the 1-1/2 yard line. The Big Ten announced the next day that officials had made an error on the ball spot following the penalty on the two-point conversion attempt.[27]

Knight ran for 103 yards on 22 carries and had three catches for 12 yards. Iamaleava completed 10 of 17 passes for 162 yards. Dieffenbach was three of six passing for 43 yards, one touchdown and did not turn the ball over. Raiola, starting in place of Dante Moore, completed six of 19 passes for 43 yards and two interceptions. He was replaced by third-string quarterback Brock Thomas, who was 11-of-15 passing for 156 yards, one touchdown and no turnovers.[23]
