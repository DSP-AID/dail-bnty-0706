The 2026 Oklahoma Sooners football team represents the University of Oklahoma in the Southeastern Conference (SEC) during the 2026 NCAA Division I FBS football season, the 132nd season for the Oklahoma Sooners. They are led by fifth-year head coach Brent Venables and play their home games at Gaylord Family Oklahoma Memorial Stadium in Norman, Oklahoma.

Schedule

[edit]

Oklahoma and the SEC announced the 2026 conference schedule on December 11, 2025.[1] The 2026 schedule consists of six home games, five away games, and one neutral-site game in the regular season. The Sooners will host two non-conference games against UTEP and New Mexico and will travel to Michigan.[2] The Sooners will host four SEC conference opponents against Kentucky, South Carolina, Ole Miss and Texas A&M, and will travel to four SEC conference opponents against Georgia, Mississippi State, Florida, and Missouri. The Sooners will face Texas in Dallas at the Cotton Bowl in the 122nd Red River Rivalry.

On September 4, No. 10 Oklahoma defeated UTEP 51–0 before 84,315 fans at Owen Field.[6]

The game started with a 5:39 drive that ended in a 1-yard touchdown run by Lloyd Avant. On the next drive, UTEP went three-and-out, setting up an 88-yard punt return touchdown by Isaiah Sategna III. On UTEP's next drive, they reached the Oklahoma 15-yard line before missing a wide right field goal. On the next drive, Oklahoma scored on a 40-yard pass from John Mateer to Rocky Beers, and led 21–0 at the end of the first quarter. The second quarter was stagnant, with UTEP missing another field goal to start the second. Oklahoma would only score two field goals in the second by Tate Sandell. UTEP started the second half with the ball, but turned it over on a forced fumble by Peyton Bowen. Oklahoma scored two passing touchdowns in the third, including a 48-yard pass to Trell Harris. Oklahoma tacked on a late third-quarter field goal by Liam Evans to go up 44–0. Oklahoma scored its last touchdown on a three-yard rushing touchdown by backup quarterback Whitt Newbauer.[7]

The 51–0 win over UTEP marked the first time since 2024 that Oklahoma scored 50 or more points, when they defeated Maine 59–14 on November 2, 2024.

On September 12, No. 11 Oklahoma fell to Michigan 17–10 before 111,240 fans at Michigan Stadium.[8]

Bryce Underwood scored the first touchdown on a 38-yard run late in the first quarter. Michigan scored again late in the second with a 40-yard field goal by Trey Butkowski, leading 10–0 at halftime. Michigan received the ball after the half and went three-and-out. Oklahoma scored its first touchdown eight plays into its first second-half drive on a 22-yard pass from John Mateer to Mackenzie Alleyne. Oklahoma stopped Michigan again, and Tate Sandell tied it early in the fourth quarter with a 34-yard field goal. With 8:49 remaining, John Mateer threw an interception to Jyaire Hill, who returned it to the Oklahoma six-yard line. Jordan Marshall scored a one-yard rushing touchdown two plays later, putting Michigan up 17–10.[9] Oklahoma failed to score on three successive drives, including a punt, a turnover on downs, and a drive that ended with a sack by Michigan defensive end Nate Marshall as time expired.

Oklahoma's loss to Michigan was the first time the school lost to the team after two previous meetings. This game also marked the first time Oklahoma was shut out in any half since October 11, 2025, when Texas beat Oklahoma 23–6 in the Red River Rivalry that year.

On September 19, No. 24 Oklahoma defeated New Mexico 14–6 in a close match before 83,423 fans at Owen Field.[10]

Oklahoma got the ball to start, but it ended in an interception three plays in. Neither team scored in the first quarter. Oklahoma struck first with a 29-yard pass from John Mateer to Hayden Hansen for a touchdown. New Mexico tacked on two field goals late in the second quarter, helped by an Oklahoma punt deep in their own territory and another John Mateer interception with just over three minutes left. Oklahoma drove to the New Mexico 23-yard line with under a minute left, but chose to chew clock and set up a 41-yard field goal attempt by Tate Sandell, which missed to the right. Many fans booed the team during the first half and heading into the locker room, including after John Matter's two interceptions and the poor sequence of plays that ended the first half with the missed field goal.[11]

New Mexico got the ball to start the second half down 6–7. New Mexico reached the Oklahoma 48-yard line, but a sack and a holding penalty pushed them back, forcing them to punt. On the ensuing drive, Oklahoma scored a touchdown on a five-play drive, capped by a 53-yard pass from John Mateer to Isaiah Sategna III.[12] It was the last time either team scored. Oklahoma's two fourth-quarter drives ended in punts. New Mexico's last drive looked promising after driving into Oklahoma's red zone and converting on three third downs. With under three minutes remaining, David Stone stopped them on fourth down. Oklahoma exhausted New Mexico's timeouts, got a first down, and knelt the ball to end the game.

The matchup marked the first time New Mexico and Oklahoma met in football since 1935. Oklahoma's win over New Mexico 14–6 also marked the lowest amount of points Oklahoma scored in a game they won since October 9, 2004, when they defeated Texas 12–0 in that year's Red River Rivalry.

On September 26, Oklahoma lost to No. 2 Georgia in a blowout 41–13 before 93,033 fans at Sanford Stadium.[13]

Neither team scored on their first possession. Georgia scored first with a two-yard rushing touchdown by Chauncey Bowens to lead 7–0 late in the first quarter. Georgia scored again early in the second quarter on a long 35-yard pass from Gunner Stockton to Talyn Taylor. On Oklahoma's next drive, they reached the Georgia two-yard line on third-and-goal. However, John Mateer fumbled the ball as he was attempting to pass on a play-action power-o-play: the ball was recovered by Chris Cole; he returned it for an 85-yard fumble-recovery touchdown to lead 21–0. Oklahoma would end the first half with two more turnovers by John Mateer, including an interception to KJ Bolden and another fumble recovery by Chris Cole; the latter led to another rushing touchdown by Chauncey Bowens to lead 28–0 at halftime.[14]

Early in the second quarter, Oklahoma scored its first touchdown on a 17-yard pass from John Matter to Rocky Beers; Omarion Robinson intercepted the ball at the Georgia 35-yard line on the previous Georgia drive to assist the drive. The ensuing two-point conversion by Lloyd Avant was unsuccessful on his rushing attempt. Georgia added two field goals by Peyton Woodring in the second half, and a 54-yard rushing touchdown by Dwight Phillips Jr. Oklahoma scored its last points late in the fourth quarter on a 14-yard passing touchdown from John Mateer to Manny Choice.[15]

The game marked the second meeting between the two schools and the first between them since the 2018 Rose Bowl. It was the Sooners' worst loss since the 31-point defeat by Texas on October 12, 2024. Brent Venables considered benching John Mateer.[16]

On October 10, at the 122nd Red River Rivalry matchup, Oklahoma upset No. 1 Texas 27–24 in a highly competitive game before 92,100 fans in the Cotton Bowl.[17]

Oklahoma started with possession and went three-and-out. Neither team achieved a first down on their first two drives. After Oklahoma's third drive, Texas got the ball on a punt and scored on a one-play drive that culminated in a passing touchdown from Arch Manning to Cam Coleman to lead 7-0. After three more drives, Oklahoma scored on a blocked punt in the end zone to tie it 7–7; Danny Okoye blocked the punt, and Dakoda Fields recovered it just short of the end line.[18] Texas scored on the next drive on a 34-yard pass from Arch Manning to Ryan Wingo to go ahead 14–7. On Oklahoma's next drive, they failed to score as Tate Sandell missed a 53-yard field goal. On Texas' final first-half drive, they drove inside Oklahoma's red zone, but were stopped at the five-yard line, forcing Gianni Spetic to kick a 23-yard field goal to lead 17–7 just before halftime.

Texas got the ball to start the second half, but they failed to score on their first three drives: two were three-and-outs. After Texas' first drive, Oklahoma cut the lead to 10–17 after Tate Sandell made a 53-yard field goal. During Texas' third punt of the half, several Texas players tried to push Isaiah Sategna III on the ball, causing a scuffle. Oklahoma then tied the game early in the fourth quarter on a two-yard rushing touchdown by Lloyd Avant.[19] Several Texas penalties aided the scoring drive, including a roughing-the-passer penalty by Rasheem Biles on a double pass play that was intercepted and called back, as well as a pass-interference penalty by Kade Phillips when Oklahoma could have been stopped on third-and-goal. Oklahoma took a 24–17 lead with 10:07 remaining on a 42-yard interception return for a touchdown by Peyton Bowen.[20] Texas responded on the next drive with a 15-yard pass from Arch Manning to Jermaine Bishop Jr. for a touchdown, tying the game. Texas had a chance to take the lead with a field goal with two minutes remaining, but it missed wide right.

After another unsuccessful Oklahoma drive, Texas got the ball back on a punt with under a minute remaining to attempt a game-winning drive. However, Arch Manning threw another interception to Courtland Guillory with 19 seconds remaining. John Mateer completed two crucial passes to Isaiah Sategna III, getting Oklahoma into field goal range. Tate Sandell won the game with a 47-yard field goal down the middle.[21]

Oklahoma's defeat of No. 1 Texas was the first time Oklahoma defeated Texas when they were ranked No. 1. It was also the first win for Oklahoma against a No. 1 team since they defeated Missouri 38–17 in the 2007 Big 12 Championship Game on December 1, 2007.[22][17] The game also marked the first time Oklahoma scored a touchdown against Texas since Nic Anderson last did so in 2023.

On October 17, following the historic upset over Texas, Oklahoma fell to No. 15 Kentucky in a close 21–13 game before 83,218 fans at Owen Field in its home SEC opener.[23]

Kentucky got the ball first and scored about halfway through the first quarter on a two-yard pass from Kenny Minchey to Willie Rodriguez for a touchdown. Oklahoma responded on the next drive with another long drive that ended in a 44-yard field goal by Tate Sandell, cutting the Wildcat's lead to 7–3. Oklahoma had a chance to kick another field goal on their drive to cut the lead to 7–6, but a fake field goal was attempted; Tate Sandell rolled out left before the snap, and Jacob Ulrich attempted to do a jump pass to Rocky Beers, but it was misthrown and ended in an interception by Jordan Castell.[24] Oklahoma got the ball back after another Kentucky punt and capitalized on a 20-yard pass from John Mateer to Rocky Beers a half-minute before halftime to lead 10–7.

Oklahoma received the ball to start the second half. Neither team scored on their first possession and punted. Oklahoma extended the lead to 13–7 on a 26-yard field goal by Tate Sandell. On Oklahoma's next drive, they had a chance to extend the lead late in the third quarter when they reached the Kentucky 38-yard line; Mateer threw an interception that Mi'Quise Humphrey-Grace broke up, and Ty Bryant returned it to the Oklahoma 44-yard line. Kentucky capitalized on the interception with another passing touchdown: Kinney Michey to Willie Rodriguez for 12 yards to take a 14–13 lead. Despite a goal-line stand by Oklahoma just over halfway through the fourth quarter, Oklahoma's offense failed to score on its last three drives. John Mateer threw two interceptions on those last two drives; Jordan Castell returned the first for a 41-yard interception return for a touchdown, giving the Wildcats a 21–13 lead. The second interception effectively ended the game, as Kentucky knelt the ball to end it.[25]

The game between Kentucky and Oklahoma marked the first time the teams had met since 1982.[26]

On October 24, Oklahoma beat Mississippi State 23–15 in front of 49,224 fans at Davis Wade Stadium.

Mississippi received the ball to start the game and went three-and-out on their first two possessions. Oklahoma scored on its first drive with a 42-yard field goal by Tate Sandell; Mississippi State's first drive ended with a short 13-yard punt by Ethan Pulliam. On the last play of Mississippi State's third drive, Kamario Taylor suffered a season-ending turf toe injury as he was trying to run beyond and outside the passing pocket to try and convert on 4th & 5, but ended up being sacked by Owen Heinecke for a loss of two and a turnover on downs. Oklahoma had a chance to extend the lead on the next drive at the start of the second quarter, but Tate Sandell missed a 45-yard field goal. Mississippi State had a chance late in the second quarter to score a touchdown inside Oklahoma's red zone, but Billy Puckett fumbled on a two-yard rush at the Oklahoma four-yard line. Oklahoma capitalized on the turnover to end the first half with a 36-yard passing touchdown from John Mateer to Isaiah Sategna III, leading 10–0.

Oklahoma scored on their first possession of the second half on a 32-yard touchdown pass from John Mateer to Rocky Beers, leading 17–0. Mississippi State scored on its second drive of the second half with an 18-yard double pass from tight end Sam West to wide receiver Camden Capehart. Oklahoma responded with a 30-yard field goal by Tate Sandell. Oklahoma then stopped Mississippi State on their next three drives, including an interception by Peyton Bowen in their end zone on the last. However, Oklahoma failed to score after the first two Mississippi stops, with Tate Sandell missing two consecutive field goals. After the Mississippi interception, Tate Sandell made a 38-yard field goal to push the lead to 23–7. Mississippi State responded with a 42-yard pass from Billy Puckett to Sanfrisco Magee and a two-point conversion to cut the deficit to 23–15. Mississippi State could not stop Oklahoma on their next drive after exhausting their timeouts, and Oklahoma got a first down, ending the game after they knelt the ball.

The matchup between Oklahoma and Mississippi State marked the first time that the schools played each other.

On October 31, South Carolina beat Oklahoma 42–25 in a blowout before 83,189 fans at Owen Field.

South Carolina got the ball first, and both teams punted on their opening drives. South Carolina scored first with a 15-yard rushing touchdown by LaNorris Sellers. Oklahoma tied the game on South Carolina's fourth drive via a 30-yard interception return by Kip Lewis. After that, South Carolina scored touchdowns on its final three first-half possessions: two passing touchdowns by Sellers and one rushing touchdown by Christian Clark. Oklahoma scored only a 52-yard field goal by Tate Sandell after South Carolina scored their third touchdown. Before the field goal, on an injury timeout, many fans, especially the student section, chanted for Oklahoma offensive coordinator Ben Arbuckle to be fired.

The only third-quarter scoring play came on an 11-yard rushing touchdown by LaNorris Sellers. Oklahoma scored its first offensive points early in the fourth quarter on a deep 34-yard passing touchdown from John Mateer to Ivan Carreon. South Carolina responded on the next drive, on third down, with a deep pass from LaNorris Sellers to Mazeo Bennett Jr. for a 79-yard touchdown for their last points of the game. Oklahoma scored on its next two drives with a 34-yard field goal by Tate Sandell and another passing touchdown from John Mateer to Ivan Carreon for 10 yards. Both of Oklahoma's fourth-quarter passing touchdowns ended with failed two-point conversions on John Mateer pass attempts. Oklahoma had a chance to score with just over half a minute remaining, but it ended in a turnover on downs at the South Carolina one-yard line on a rush attempt by Lloyd Avant.

The game between South Carolina and Oklahoma was their third meeting. Oklahoma’s 42–25 loss was its first by more than two scores since October 19, 2024, when South Carolina beat Oklahoma that year 35–9. Two days after the game, Oklahoma dismissed Ben Arbuckle in his second season as offensive coordinator due to a lack of consistency and development.
